Readability: review and proposal

This page is about readability: can you read things, and can you find them at a glance? It doesn't judge whether the features are worth having. Three reviewers on three different models looked at the app's screens: someone doing quick find-it tasks, a legibility specialist and a layout specialist. An auditor then checked my ratings, found real problems with my first proposal, and I reworked it.

What it's about: your words, “something preventing my eyes from locking into each thing”. It happens on both your screens, every session, and it slows you down without losing any work. One round, then this proposal. Nothing is merged until you say so.

Why your eyes don't lock on

HIGHNothing anchors the eye. The section headings (“Size and rotation”, “Page size”) were the faintest, smallest text in the panel, weaker than the labels under them. So you read the column from the top every time. Two of the three reviewers called this the main cause.
HIGHA haze of faint grey. Counts, units (wide, high, px, %), menu shortcuts and menu headings were a light grey at 3 to 1 against the panel. The usual minimum for text is 4.5 to 1. Each is readable alone; together they blur.
MEDIUMSmall type. Most text was 12.5 px and captions 11 px. On your 14" laptop at 100% scaling that's about 2 mm tall.
MEDIUMLayers and the objects in them look alike. This is where the find-it reviewer slowed down.
LOWThe chosen colour swatch was hard to spot; on the blue swatch its blue ring all but vanished. The Layer dropdown drew in the wrong font. With a tool picked, its settings ran straight into the page settings.

The proposal, before and after

In numbers:
  • Text 12.5 → 13.5 px, captions 11 → 12, titles 14.5 → 15.5, keyboard-key labels a step up.
  • All secondary text in one readable grey (5.8 to 1).
  • Headings dark and semibold.
  • The light grey is kept only for things you don't read: drag grips and the names of hidden layers, which should look switched off.

Your decisions

  1. Your laptop's display scaling. What is it set to? At 100% everything there is about 40% smaller than on your 24". Setting it to 125% would help every app, this one included.
  2. The type and colour set above. My recommendation: take it.
  3. The band behind layer rows. It's its own change, so it can be dropped. My recommendation: take it.
  4. Field outlines. The input boxes' borders are very faint (1.4 to 1). Darkening them would make the boxes stand out more, at the cost of a slightly busier panel. My recommendation: leave them for now and see whether the rest fixes the haze.
  5. The shortcuts window. Two reviewers found it dense; the third found it the easiest screen in the app. My recommendation: leave it.
Also fixed, separately

The page now stays centred when the window grows just after opening. That's the off-centre page you reported.

Checked and left alone
  • The swatches being the loudest thing in the panel. Colours are meant to be seen.
  • The space between sections. It's already larger than the space between rows.
  • The current-layer bar. It's already 3 px.
  • The pale “Reset to default”. That's how a disabled button looks, and it's only disabled when there's nothing to reset.
Glossary
  • Contrast 4.5 to 1: how much darker text is than its background; 4.5 to 1 is the usual minimum for small text.
  • Anchor: the thing your eye lands on first in a group, usually its heading.
  • Scaling: the display setting that makes everything on screen bigger (100%, 125%, 150%).
